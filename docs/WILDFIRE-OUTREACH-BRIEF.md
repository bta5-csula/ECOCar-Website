## Wildfire project direction

Cal State LA will work with Stellantis on the development of an electrified Jeep Cherokee for off-road applications. In response to significant wildfire impacts in Southern California, the team selected a Wildfire Command Vehicle concept focused on search-and-rescue and emergency-response applications. The direction extends workforce-development and vehicle-electrification experience into an application relevant to clean mobility and emergency-response needs in Southern California.

The team also wants to investigate a fire-resilient reconnaissance drone that could extend situational awareness and connect with related engineering and entrepreneurship work.

## Problems supported by the supplied material

- Wildfires can block or damage roads and rail routes needed for evacuation and emergency response.
- Smoke, loss of visibility, power interruptions, and communications disruption can make operations more difficult.
- Post-fire effects can continue after the flames, including damaged infrastructure and cascading transportation hazards.
- Responders need timely, reliable field information while conditions are changing.
- Aerial sensing, including drones and infrared capabilities, is a relevant area for investigation.

These findings define problems and research directions. They do not prove that the team's vehicle already solves them.

## Stakeholder discovery

Before settling vehicle functions, the team should speak with affected communities and fire-service professionals. Useful questions include:

- What information is hardest to obtain during a wildfire response?
- Which transportation and access failures most affect operations?
- What vehicle functions would be useful in the field, and what would create added burden?
- What power, communications, safety, training, maintenance, and interoperability constraints must be considered?
- When could a reconnaissance drone help, and what operational or regulatory limits matter?

## Claim guardrails

- Use language such as **exploring**, **studying**, **investigating**, **proposed**, or **in development** for unvalidated capabilities.
- Do not claim that the vehicle prevents wildfires, guarantees access, replaces first responders, or has been field-tested.
- Do not publish firetruck sales, firefighter counts, attendance totals, or traffic-growth claims without a defined scope and a verifiable source.
- Keep proposals visibly separate from tested results in future updates.
